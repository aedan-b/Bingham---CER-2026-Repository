The quantitative side of investing is well studied, with many recognizable phrases such as herding behavior and fear of missing out bias. Yet, few existing experiments look towards the qualitative side, digging into the why behind investing decisions. This paper puts a dent in that, describing that team-based investing is a process in which sequential and frequently subconscious checkpoints are reached before a buy or sell decision is made. Two factors make this particularly unique, (1) a period of heightened market volatility and (2) the study group consisting of college students. Under these conditions, the conclusions reached point towards teams with consistent investing principles and frequent communication resulting in higher returns.
